From safety nets to lifecycle social insurance for Bangladesh

A professional knowledge hub for the Social Security Unit to track current assistance programmes, emerging insurance schemes, laws, responsible ministries, ILO standards, and actuarial policy scenarios.

Strategic framing

  • NSSS direction: consolidate fragmented programmes and move toward lifecycle social protection.
  • Current proof: EIS pilot is operating for the export-oriented RMG sector, with income-replacement top-ups for permanent disability and survivors.
  • Next policy frontier: maternity, sickness and unemployment protection under NSIS, alongside contributory old-age coverage through UPS.

Legal and international standards

ILO conventions and Bangladesh policy anchors

C102 — Social Security Minimum Standards

Flagship convention covering nine branches: medical care, sickness, unemployment, old age, employment injury, family, maternity, invalidity and survivors’ benefits.

C121 — Employment Injury Benefits

Reference standard for employment injury compensation: periodical earnings-related payments and survivors’ benefits; core standard for EIS nationalization.

C183 — Maternity Protection

Minimum 14 weeks maternity leave with adequate cash and medical benefits; useful benchmark for pooled maternity insurance.

C168 / C128

C168 frames unemployment protection and employment promotion; C128 frames invalidity, old-age and survivors’ benefits.

Bangladesh has ratified several labour conventions, including equality-of-treatment social security standards, but C121 and C128 are listed by ILO NORMLEX Bangladesh country profile as not ratified. Ratification strategy should follow legal readiness, actuarial sustainability and administrative capacity.

Insurance policy models

Four policy models used in the scenario calculator

These are design options, not approved contribution schedules. The status table above contains current official amounts; calculator rates below are editable assumptions for scenario testing.

1

National Employment Injury Scheme

Goal: replace fragmented employer liability with a no-fault, employer-financed, risk-pooled EII.

  • Coverage: formal workers first; phased expansion to EPZs, leather, construction, transport and high-risk sectors.
  • Benefits: medical care, temporary incapacity, permanent disability pension, survivors’ pension, rehabilitation and return-to-work.
  • Finance: the current pilot uses voluntary brand contributions equal to 0.019% of annual RMG export value. A future statutory employer premium has not been approved and should be set through actuarial valuation and sector-risk analysis.
  • Lesson: Malaysia SOCSO’s employer-financed employment injury branch and Korea’s employer-funded industrial accident insurance.
2

National Maternity Benefit Insurance

Goal: pool maternity costs to protect women workers and reduce employer discrimination risk.

  • Coverage: wage-employed women first; voluntary/self-employed window supported by state subsidy.
  • Benefits: model wage replacement for up to the current Labour Act duration of 120 days, while testing adequacy against ILO C183 and coordinating antenatal/postnatal services, job protection and lactation support.
  • Finance: charge the maternity contribution to covered female workers only. No official NSIS premium exists; the calculator uses an editable scenario rate, with public subsidy to be tested for low-income and informal workers.
  • Lesson: Korea finances maternity leave benefits through employment insurance; ILO C183 sets the adequacy benchmark.
3

Unemployment Insurance + Employment Services

Goal: provide temporary income security while linking workers to jobs, training and re-employment.

  • Coverage: formal private workers with contribution history; voluntary extension to platform/self-employed later.
  • Benefits: test alternative durations and replacement rates together with job-search support, training and re-employment incentives. No national benefit schedule has been approved.
  • Finance: a shared payroll contribution may be tested, but Bangladesh has not approved a national unemployment-insurance rate. The calculator rate is an editable assumption only.
  • Lesson: Malaysia’s EIS charges 0.4% split equally and links benefits with job-search and training.
4

Universal Pension / Old-Age Contributory Model

Goal: build a contributory pension pillar that complements tax-financed old-age allowance.

  • Coverage: mandate Progoti-type employer-worker contributions for formal private sector; retain Samata subsidy for poor informal workers.
  • Benefits: lifetime pension from retirement age; survivor/nominee protection; indexed minimum pension for low contributors.
  • Finance: use the official UPS fixed monthly contribution schedules for Probash, Progoti, Surakkha and Samata; any payroll-mandated occupational tier would require separate legislation and actuarial design.
  • Lesson: Korea’s National Pension uses shared employer/employee contributions; Malaysia EPF shows scale but requires adequacy safeguards.

Implementation roadmap

Proposed sequencing

Phase 1: 2026–2028

Legal drafting, national NSIS governance board, actuarial baseline, EIS statutory conversion, single registry/payroll data linkage, pilot maternity pooling in RMG/export sectors.

Phase 2: 2029–2031

Formal private-sector expansion, employment service platform, unemployment insurance pilot in selected sectors, UPS mandate for larger employers, unified contribution collection.

Phase 3: 2032 onward

Multi-branch Social Insurance Institution, informal worker subsidized windows, indexation rules, sector risk-rating, convention ratification roadmap, public actuarial valuation every 3 years.

Calculator definitions

Input and output definitions for scenario modelling

These notes explain each calculator parameter and result heading. The calculator is a simplified scenario model, so replace default values with administrative, payroll, claims and demographic data before using results for policy costing.

Scenario Timeline

Workers
Total number of workers covered by the projection. This is the active contributor base used for payroll, expected claims and contribution income.
Monthly wage, Tk
Average monthly wage per covered worker in Taka. The calculator multiplies this by workers and 12 months to estimate annual payroll.
Start year
The first calendar year in the projection table.
Years
Number of years included in the projection. The default 5-year timeline creates 5 annual rows for each scheme.
Worker growth, %
Expected annual percentage growth or decline in covered workers. It changes the future payroll and expected claims base.
Wage growth, %
Expected annual growth in average wages. It increases projected payroll and wage-linked benefits.
Fund return, %
Annual investment return applied to each scheme's accumulated amount before adding new contributions and subtracting annual costs.
Admin margin, %
Additional loading applied to benefit cost for administration, contingency and reserve needs. For example, 15% raises a Tk 100 benefit cost to Tk 115.

Employment Injury Scheme

Claims, % workers/year
Expected annual employment injury claims as a percentage of covered workers.
Benefit, wage months
Average cost per injury claim expressed as months of average wage. Higher months represent more severe or longer-duration benefits.
Premium, % payroll
Contribution income collected for EIS as a percentage of covered payroll.

Maternity Benefit Insurance

Female share, % workers
Share of covered workers who are women. The maternity claim base is calculated from this share.
Birth rate, % female workers
Expected annual maternity claims as a percentage of covered female workers.
Leave, weeks
Number of paid maternity leave weeks included in the cost formula.
Replacement, %
Share of the worker's wage replaced during maternity leave. A value of 100 means full wage replacement.
Premium, % female-worker payroll
Contribution income collected for maternity insurance as a percentage of covered female-worker payroll only. Male workers are not charged this maternity premium.

Unemployment Insurance

Claims, % workers
Expected annual unemployment claims as a percentage of covered workers.
Duration, months
Number of months of unemployment cash benefit included in the model.
Replacement, %
Share of monthly wage paid during the unemployment benefit period.
Service cost, Tk
Additional non-cash support cost per unemployment claim, such as job matching, training, counselling or case management.
Premium, % payroll
Contribution income collected for unemployment insurance as a percentage of covered payroll.

Old-Age Social Insurance

Retiree ratio, % workers
Number of pensioners relative to active covered workers. It approximates the dependency load on the active contributor base.
Replacement, % wage
Pension benefit level as a percentage of average wage. A value of 25 means annual pension cost equals 25% of annual average wage for each retiree.
Contribution, % payroll
Contribution income collected for old-age social insurance as a percentage of covered payroll.

Output Results

Projection timeline
The start and end years covered by the current scenario.
Final-year payroll
Total covered payroll in the last projection year, after applying worker and wage growth assumptions.
Policies modeled
Number of schemes included in the calculator scenario.
Scenario status
Shows whether the visible results have been calculated from current inputs or whether inputs were changed after the last calculation.
Final-year cost
The scheme's annual cost in the last projection year, including the admin margin.
Final cost
The annual cost shown in each projection row for that year, including the admin margin.
Accumulated amount
The projected reserve balance after adding contributions, subtracting costs and applying fund return through that year.
Claim rate
The claim-rate assumption used to estimate expected claims for the scheme.
Final-year claims
Expected number of claims in the last projection year.
Required premium rate
The contribution-base rate required to keep the final accumulated reserve at zero or above across the full projection timeline. Maternity uses female-worker payroll as the contribution base; other schemes use total covered payroll.
Contribution or premium rate
The user-set contribution-base rate used to calculate contribution income for the scheme.
Benefit setup
A short summary of the benefit design assumptions used for the scheme, such as months, weeks or wage replacement percentage.
Cumulative cost
Total scheme cost from the first projection year through the last projection year.
Year
The calendar year represented by a projection-table row.
Claims
Expected number of claims in the listed year.
Contributions
Contribution income collected for the scheme in the listed year.
